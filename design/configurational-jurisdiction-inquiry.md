# Configurational Jurisdiction Inquiry

*Initial design prototype for investigating authority, corrective reach and reversibility*

**Working research design · July 2026**

> **Design proposition.** A corrective signal does not change a system merely because it exists. It must remain visible and meaningful, travel across relevant relations, reach a position with alteration power, and return to the configuration strongly enough to affect what happens next. Configurational Jurisdiction Inquiry makes that movement inspectable and contestable.

## 1. Purpose and status

Configurational Jurisdiction Inquiry is a proposed visual and participatory method for investigating how socio-technical arrangements acquire authority, distribute burdens and respond to correction over time.

The method is being developed through the Robodebt case. It is an initial research prototype rather than a validated professional instrument. Its operations, categories and visual conventions remain open to empirical revision.

The method addresses situations in which:

- no participant possesses a complete system view;
- evidence is partial, disputed, delayed or strategically selected;
- apparently separate cases may share an authority-producing relation;
- feedback exists but does not necessarily change that relation;
- institutional and technical operations reinforce one another over time; and
- some consequences become progressively harder to prevent, reverse or repair.

Its purpose is not to produce a single authoritative map. It constructs and compares **position-specific provisional configurations**, preserves unresolved differences and tests what would allow knowledge from different positions to revise both the inquiry and the arrangement being investigated.

## 2. Central research object

The unit of inquiry is a **successive configuration**: the changing set of relations through which purposes, powers, data, formal operations, evidence, workflows, people, burdens and corrective capacities acquire force together.

An algorithm is therefore not examined as an isolated technical object. The inquiry asks:

- Why was this operation available here?
- What institutional goal or pressure made it useful?
- What evidence did an intended action require?
- Through which rule or permission did its output become actionable?
- Which later actions reinforced that authority?
- Who experienced the resulting burden?
- What could different corrective positions actually observe and change?

The inquiry represents change as movement between configurations:

```text
Formed conditions
       ↓
Goals, pressures and available operations
       ↓
Authority-producing hinge
       ↓
Institutional action and distributed effects
       ↓
Responses, complaints, correction and resistance
       ↓
Amplification, absorption or reconfiguration
       ↓
Subsequent configuration
```

Every cycle may add precedent, data, investment, dependency, workload, justification and apparent legitimacy. A response can also change the configuration, but this is not assumed to happen automatically.

## 3. Position-specific configurations

Affected people, frontline staff, technical staff, policy teams, managers, legal advisers, courts, advocates, journalists and oversight bodies may encounter different boundaries, beginnings, objects, timescales and possibilities for action.

For each relevant position, the inquiry records:

| Question | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| What enters this position? | Information, instructions, cases, demands and constraints |
| What becomes visible here? | The objects and effects this position can perceive |
| What remains outside its view? | Blind spots, exclusions and dependencies |
| What can this position do? | Available operations, discretion and alteration power |
| What burden does it carry? | Evidentiary, administrative, financial, ethical or personal load |
| Where can its knowledge travel? | Reporting, review, escalation and public channels |
| What can defeat or absorb its response? | Delay, fragmentation, translation, hierarchy or lack of standing |

These accounts are not treated as fragments waiting to be assembled by an all-seeing investigator. Each position can reveal a different operative configuration. The shared map records their relations and incompatibilities without assuming that one position owns the whole.

Participation is consequential when it changes at least one of the following:

- the inquiry boundary;
- the represented object;
- the sequence or connection being investigated;
- the account of burden or effect;
- an evidence request;
- the location of an intervention; or
- the next version of the design.

## 4. Principal visual instrument

The **Configurational Transformation Map** is the method's principal visual instrument. It is a revisable research surface, not an illustration prepared after the analysis.

The map represents:

- formed institutional conditions;
- goals and changing pressures;
- available technical and administrative operations;
- authority-producing hinges;
- actionable objects and institutional consequences;
- position-specific burdens and responses;
- corrective channels and their practical reach;
- accumulated momentum;
- stress points and power vulnerabilities;
- information gaps and competing hypotheses;
- reversibility frontiers; and
- evidenced changes between successive configurations.

Every relation should carry an evidence status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Observed | Directly supported by available evidence |
| Reported | Described by a participant or source but not independently verified |
| Inferred | Best current explanation of connected observations |
| Anticipated | Plausible prospective development |
| Disputed | Supported and contested by different accounts |
| Withheld or unavailable | Relevant information is known to be inaccessible |
| Unknown | The inquiry cannot presently determine the relation |
| Defeated | Evidence has displaced an earlier hypothesis |

The map should preserve earlier versions. An evidence-linked change ledger records why a relation was added, removed, redirected or given a different status.

## 5. Core analytical operations

### 5.1 Establish the formed field

Record what has already taken form before the focal decision or algorithm appears:

- statutory purposes and institutional powers;
- inherited classifications and evidentiary standards;
- datasets and technical infrastructure;
- ordinary algorithmic repertoire;
- political, fiscal, organisational and media pressure;
- staff capability and operational dependencies;
- complaint, review, suspension and alteration pathways; and
- earlier controversies, commitments and public narratives.

This provides an evidence-bounded account of institutional disposition. It does not claim that these conditions determine one inevitable outcome.

### 5.2 Construct a possibility map

Institutions do not possess infinitely many actions or algorithms. Existing data, infrastructure, law, procurement, expertise and routine make some operations easier to select and implement than others.

The inquiry asks:

> Under these formed conditions, pressures and goals, which available operations become comparatively convenient, and what movements could they enable?

| Formed condition | Available operation | Possible movement | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fiscal recovery pressure and historical income data | Matching and averaging | Scalable discrepancy objects become actionable | Specifications, decision records, pilots and alternatives |
| Throughput targets and limited verification capacity | Thresholding and automated routing | Cases move faster than individual verification | Workflow, staffing and exception records |
| Established noncompliance categories | Classification and default rules | Ambiguity shifts toward recipient response obligations | Rules, notices, guidance and review outcomes |
| Fragmented complaint handling | Case-level correction | Local relief occurs without common-relation revision | Complaint coding and escalation history |

The output is a conditional possibility envelope, not a prediction or allegation.

### 5.3 Locate authority-producing hinges

An **authority-producing hinge** is a transition at which a contingent representation or operation gains sufficient institutional standing to organise subsequent action.

The inquiry tests the following provisional sequence:

1. an institutional disposition and changing pressure favour a goal or action;
2. the contemplated action creates a demand for authorising evidence;
3. available data and a formal operation bridge an evidentiary gap;
4. a rule, permission or workflow admits the constructed output as actionable;
5. repeated use and institutional investment reinforce it; and
6. evidentiary and corrective burdens move toward affected or less powerful positions.

The sequence is a hypothesis to test against evidence. It does not establish that an actor selected the operation for an improper purpose.

### 5.4 Trace temporal operations

The inquiry records how time participates in producing the actionable object:

- averaging or apportionment;
- windowing and period selection;
- sequencing and synchronisation;
- deadlines and response periods;
- look-back duration;
- refresh and review cycles;
- delay, decay and loss of evidence; and
- the timing of escalation, suspension and repair.

It asks whether institutional time and affected people's time have unequal operational effects.

### 5.5 Track momentum and unequal transmission

For every significant action or response, record:

- what relation it strengthens or weakens;
- what becomes easier afterwards;
- what precedent, dependency or justification it adds;
- which positions can perceive the change;
- whether it travels toward authority or correction;
- where its meaning changes; and
- whether it affects the next configuration.

A complaint, reversal or warning is not counted as system feedback merely because it occurred. The inquiry follows whether it retained enough coherence and force to reach the common production relation.

### 5.6 Identify stress points and power vulnerabilities

A stress point is a relation where pressure, contradiction, workload or burden is accumulating. A power vulnerability is a relation that can be exploited to extend, conceal or protect authority.

The same relation may provide both corrective leverage and opportunity for abuse. Every proposed intervention therefore receives two readings:

- **corrective reading:** how could this relation interrupt harm or extend corrective reach?
- **adversarial reading:** how could it be captured, gamed, bypassed or used to suppress contrary information?

### 5.7 Mark reversibility frontiers

The inquiry distinguishes consequences that remain:

- preventable;
- interruptible;
- reversible;
- restorable only through additional action;
- compensable but not reversible; or
- effectively irreversible.

An intervention is evaluated against the frontier existing when it becomes available. Cancelling an output may not restore lost time, health, evidence, opportunity or institutional trust.

## 6. Working under incomplete information

Partial observability is treated as a normal research condition. Absence of evidence does not establish absence of a relation.

When a decision process is hidden, **blind-spot triangulation** compares:

1. what entered the arrangement;
2. its visible purposes and pressures;
3. the operations and resources available;
4. the action or output that appeared;
5. the effects it was positioned to produce; and
6. later explanations, changes or concealment behaviour.

This generates one or more bounded hypotheses about the missing transition. Each hypothesis must record its alternatives, confidence and the evidence that would support or defeat it.

Institutional disclosure is itself mapped as an action. A response may select a boundary, vocabulary, causal account and time period even when cooperation is genuine. Refusal, inconsistency or unexplained absence changes the evidentiary configuration but is not automatically treated as proof of wrongdoing.

## 7. Intervention classification

The method distinguishes interventions by what they can reach:

| Intervention | Object reached | Typical limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Output correction | Individual decision, debt or classification | Common production relation may remain intact |
| Channel repair | Complaint, review or information pathway | Signal may travel but still lack alteration power |
| Load reduction | Evidentiary, financial or administrative burden | Authority-producing operation may continue |
| Hinge challenge | Evidentiary permission or formal bridge | Requires access to the authorising relation |
| Momentum interruption | Repetition, scaling, investment or institutional defence | May arrive after consequences accumulate |
| Production-layer reconfiguration | Shared model, rule, workflow or authority relation | Usually requires coordinated powers across positions |
| Restoration and repair | Residual consequences and damaged capacities | Some consequences cannot be fully restored |

For each proposed intervention, ask:

1. What can it observe?
2. What relation can it change?
3. How quickly can it act?
4. What load does it create or redistribute?
5. How could it be absorbed or gamed?
6. What remains unchanged?
7. Does it increase the configuration's capacity to observe and revise itself?

## 8. Research procedure

### Stage 1 — Retrospective reconstruction

Construct successive Robodebt configurations from official reports, judgments, testimony, administrative material and affected accounts. Separate documented fact, participant report, inference and hypothesis.

### Stage 2 — Positional revision

Subject to access and ethics approval, ask affected and institutional participants to contest the map's boundaries, objects, timing, missing relations and intervention points.

### Stage 3 — Design extraction

Translate recurrent investigative needs and empirically supported transitions into the minimum set of mapping and inquiry operations.

### Stage 4 — Scenario-based testing

Replay selected transitions under cooperative, incomplete, contested and adversarial information conditions. Test evidence requests, escalation, aggregation, suspension, hinge challenges and production-layer interventions.

### Stage 5 — Revision and consolidation

Use the evidence-linked design ledger to revise the method. Retain operations only where they change the research object, improve evidentiary discipline, reveal a consequential relation or support a more proportionate intervention.

## 9. Evaluation criteria

The method will be evaluated by whether it:

- reveals relations missed by decision-centred or component-centred analysis;
- distinguishes local correction from production-layer change;
- makes uncertainty and disagreement inspectable;
- permits knowledge from non-controlling positions to revise the inquiry;
- identifies how authority and correction travel across connected scales;
- improves the timing or placement of an intervention;
- distinguishes warning from configurational change;
- remains usable without complete institutional access;
- resists unsupported attribution of intention; and
- identifies its own burdens, blind spots and capture risks.

## 10. Initial institutional application

**Prospective Configurational Oversight** applies this method to an imagined Ombudsman-facing inquiry.

The Ombudsman is treated as one situated participant and possible convener, not the owner of the system representation. Its complaint channels, own-motion powers, information access, operating timescale, public reporting and connections to suspension or alteration determine how prospective its inquiry can become.

The application tests whether recurring complaints and weak signals can be connected before complete harm is known; whether stress points and authority-producing hinges can be investigated under partial disclosure; and whether a finding can travel toward a relation capable of changing the configuration rather than remaining advisory or post hoc.

The application is described separately so that limitations of one institutional user are not mistaken for limitations of the general method.

## 11. Boundaries and defeat conditions

The method should be revised or abandoned where:

- its concepts cannot be linked to observable evidence;
- different investigators cannot reconstruct comparable relations;
- the visual map adds complexity without changing inquiry or intervention;
- position-specific participation becomes symbolic rather than revisionary;
- existing methods explain corrective reach more clearly and with less burden;
- prospective hypotheses cannot be disciplined under partial information;
- the method encourages unsupported attribution of motive; or
- its intervention proposals merely relocate burden or concentrate authority elsewhere.

The inquiry does not claim to predict institutional failure, eliminate strategic wrongdoing or produce a complete representation of a living system. Its narrower purpose is to make the formation, transmission and alteration of authority and corrective capacity more inspectable.

## 12. Relationship to the PhD project

Within the proposed PhD:

- **jurisdictional recession** names the central phenomenon;
- **Configurational Jurisdiction Inquiry** is the research and design method;
- the **Robodebt worked case** provides the foundational empirical development case; and
- **Prospective Configurational Oversight** provides the first bounded institutional application.

The method remains a prototype until its operations have been tested against the Robodebt evidence, revised through differently situated knowledge and evaluated through bounded intervention scenarios.

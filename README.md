# Jurisdictional Recession

## Reconfiguring Corrective Jurisdiction in Algorithmically Mediated Systems

This research investigates **jurisdictional recession**: a condition in which a socio-technical arrangement retains or expands its capacity to produce authoritative effects while the practical reach of challenge, correction and repair becomes comparatively restricted.

Using the Australian Robodebt scheme as its foundational case, the project develops **Configurational Jurisdiction Inquiry**—a visual and participatory method for examining how authority, burden, information and corrective capacity become differently visible and actionable across affected, operational, technical, managerial, legal and oversight positions.

The project asks how corrective knowledge can acquire enough visibility, coherence and force to alter an authority-producing configuration before its consequences become difficult or impossible to reverse.

## Key terms

- **Jurisdictional recession** is the phenomenon under investigation.
- **Configurational Jurisdiction Inquiry** is the proposed research and design method.
- **Prospective Configurational Oversight** is the method’s initial Ombudsman-facing application.
- **Reconfiguring Corrective Jurisdiction in Algorithmically Mediated Systems** is the proposed PhD project.

## Research problem

Accountability mechanisms may exist without possessing effective reach over the relations producing an authoritative outcome. A person might successfully challenge an individual decision while remaining unable to challenge the data transformation, evidentiary rule, administrative workflow, institutional incentive or technical operation that repeatedly produces similar decisions.

Feedback can therefore be present without being capable of reconfiguring the arrangement to which it responds.

The research examines how:

- institutional arrangements acquire direction and momentum;
- technical and administrative operations become authoritative;
- burden is transferred between positions;
- corrective signals are fragmented, delayed, recoded or dismissed;
- political and media conditions alter the configuration;
- authority-producing hinges make later actions harder to reverse;
- partial knowledge can still support prospective investigation and intervention.

## Proposed contribution

The project aims to produce:

1. A clearer account of jurisdictional recession as a distinct governance problem.
2. A configurational reconstruction of authority production and corrective failure in Robodebt.
3. A visual and participatory method for comparing knowledge across differently situated positions.
4. An initial design for investigating emerging arrangements under incomplete, contested or withheld information.
5. Intervention experiments examining what can restore or extend corrective reach before consequences become primarily post hoc.

## Scope

The proposed PhD does not attempt to provide a comprehensive theory of institutional failure or explain every dimension of Robodebt. It focuses on one bounded problem: how authority-producing configurations can become more extensive and forceful than the practical arrangements available to challenge, alter and repair them.

Robodebt provides the foundational retrospective case. The design method will then be developed for prospective use under partial information, with an Ombudsman-facing inquiry serving as its initial institutional application.
